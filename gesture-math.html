Gesture Math.

What if your fingers were the controller?

Gesture Math is a browser-based arithmetic game that turns hand gestures into answers. A webcam tracks the player's fingers, a short equation appears, and the player has four seconds to show the correct number.

01 / THE CONTEXT

A DIFFERENT KIND OF INPUT

Make the answer
something you do.

THE CHALLENGE

Most quick arithmetic games use buttons or a keyboard. Gesture Math experiments with a more physical interaction: answering by raising fingers in front of a camera.

THE DESIGN

Keep the rules simple, make the response immediate, and let the time pressure create a small game loop. The camera becomes the controller; the player's hand becomes the input.

05 / ENGINEERING NOTES

THE CHALLENGE

A gesture has to
become a number.

The game does not receive a number from the player. It receives camera frames and has to interpret the hand landmarks consistently enough to compare a gesture with the answer.

  • Challenge: Count extended fingers across up to two detected hands, including the thumb whose direction differs between left and right hands.
  • Decision: Use MediaPipe Hands landmarks, compare fingertip and joint positions, and account for the detected hand label when evaluating the thumb.
  • Learning: Real-time camera interaction needs a clear time limit and a predictable capture point; otherwise a changing hand pose can make the answer feel ambiguous.
